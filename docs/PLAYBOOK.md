# THE PLAYBOOK — AfterDesk

> Distilled 29 Jul 2026 from a page-by-page expedition through linear.app, ramp.com, anthropic.com, notion.com and stripe.com (live pages + published teardowns). We steal disciplines, never skins: AfterDesk stays night/paper/seam/mono.

## The founder's standing verdict

Too many words, not enough impact. Every future page change is measured against the copy laws below.

## Laws of the perfect site

**1. The artifact IS the argument. Every claim sits directly beside a real, dense, readable artifact — never an illustration, metaphor, or stock image. If a section has no artifact, the section does not exist.**

> Linear's hero is its actual issue view (IDs, labels, agent activity) under an 8-word headline; Stripe renders real checkout forms and fee cards as its only imagery ('nothing to hide'); Notion pairs every ~10 words of copy with a real screenshot; Anthropic replaces every boast with a dated, clickable document (Responsible Scaling Policy, Constitution).

**2. Claim:proof ratio is 1:1, alternating — never two claims in a row. Every benefit sentence carries or sits next to a countable number, a timestamp, or an artifact.**

> Ramp's homepage rhythm is claim→proof→claim→proof with 70,000 / 3.2x / $1M+ welded to headings; Stripe welds '2.2% average uplift' and '99.999% uptime' to every benefit; Linear pairs every abstract heading with a dense UI artifact and named metrics (60% of Ramp's merged PRs).

**3. One accent, one job, enforced by scarcity. A color means one thing sitewide, and the eye learns it because it appears almost nowhere.**

> Ramp's chartreuse appears on exactly 8 elements, all CTAs, measured across 3,000 DOM nodes; Stripe's indigo earns one filled CTA per band; Anthropic's clay is scoped to the logo slash and links; Notion's purple holds a total CTA monopoly; Linear's lavender is brand/CTA/focus only.

**4. Hierarchy is built from size, tracking, and text-tint steps — never from weight ladders or shouting bolds. Display weight caps at 600; several sites run one weight for the entire site.**

> Ramp: Lausanne at weight 400 everywhere, hierarchy by a 10→16→28→48→64 size ladder; Linear caps display at 600 and does most hierarchy with 4 white-opacity text tints; Stripe runs weight-300 display with aggressive negative tracking; Notion never exceeds 600.

**5. Elevation is surface tone and 1px hairlines, not shadows. The one deep shadow on the page is reserved for the single most important artifact.**

> Linear's hard rule is 'no drop shadows' — hover = lighter surface step; Ramp's every border is one rgba(33,33,33,0.1) hairline, and its bento is one container with gap:1px painting the seams; Anthropic stacks warm paper tones with consistent radii; Notion reserves its deep 24px/48px 'mockup shadow' exclusively for product screenshots.

**6. Motion is allowed in exactly two layers: evidence (live data that is genuinely real) and quarantined identity atmosphere. Everything else gets one orchestrated entrance, then permanent stillness. Prices, tables, text, and nav never move.**

> Ramp animates only its odometer counters and data canvases — text and layout never move; Stripe's WebGL gradient is killed the moment it leaves the viewport and its fee tables are static; Anthropic's doctrine is one staggered page-load reveal then nothing; Linear allows one entrance per section, zero loops.

**7. Numbers lead, words caption. A stat is displayed as [huge tabular figure] + [≤8-word label], never as a sentence containing a number.**

> Ramp's stat-wall grammar: 'Barry's / 400 hours / back monthly from doing expense reports'; Stripe's instrument clusters: '500M+ / API requests per day'; Notion's hyper-specific '98% of the Forbes Cloud 100' beats any 'trusted by thousands'.

**8. Dated recency is trust. A strip of timestamped, real recent activity proves the machine is alive better than any adjective.**

> Linear puts 4 dated changelog entries on the homepage as momentum-proof; Anthropic timestamps every card and renders /news as a Date|Tag|Title table; Ramp's rolling counters prove the system is running before the copy speaks.

**9. Candor is compression. Constraints, fine print, and refusals stated plainly are the cheapest trust a small operation can buy — one honest paragraph outperforms a benefits grid.**

> Anthropic's careers page openly states no internships and no feedback, and it reads as integrity; Stripe publishes a 'Prohibited businesses' page and puts a compliance asterisk in its hero; Ramp wears full bank-issuer disclosures and a support phone number in the footer.

**10. Publish the philosophy as documentation. A doctrine/process page written like a spec (numbered, dated, versioned) converts opinionated constraints into authority.**

> Linear's Method page positions the tool as a philosophy; Anthropic's homepage points at its own policies as its proof; Stripe treats docs as a first-class marketing surface, linking fee lines into them — maintained depth signals 'adults run this'.

**11. Identical repeated card anatomy makes proof scannable. One fixed formula — [name] + [one specific verb sentence] + [1-2 mono stats] — repeated without variation.**

> Stripe's case studies all follow '[Customer] does [specific thing] with Stripe' + two stats, making 12 of them scannable; Notion's story cards carry exactly one metric each ('Clay's New Default — 80+ active custom agents'); Ramp's customer wall is logo + figure + ≤8-word outcome, uniform.

**12. Structural strings live in mono with tabular numerals: IDs, prices, timestamps, section counters, category tags. Mono is the annotation voice; it makes numbers read as machine-true and keeps digits from jittering.**

> Linear sets issue IDs in mono; Ramp verified tabular-nums on all rolling counters; Stripe uses tnum on every fee table so prices never wiggle; Anthropic uses uppercase mono-style tags and '01/07' section counters.

**13. Numbered structure reads as protocol, not marketing. Numbering sections/steps in mono gives a page a table-of-contents spine without adding words.**

> Linear's homepage is five numbered chapters (1.0 Intake → 5.0 Monitor), each one heading + one artifact; Anthropic numbers careers sections '01/07' and names 7 values — structure does the explaining the copy would otherwise have to.

**14. One quarantined identity element carries all the expressiveness; everything else is two-ink monochrome discipline. The tension between one loud zone and total restraint IS the brand.**

> Stripe's gradient band is the only expressive element and never touches UI, tables, or pricing; Linear's glow is static atmosphere behind artifacts; Anthropic's entire logo is a wordmark with one slash. For AfterDesk, the seam between night and paper is this element.

**15. Minimal nav, single CTA per page, dense (but bounded) footer. Promote ≤6 ideas at the top; the nav's contents are themselves a statement of priorities — putting Pricing in the top nav is a trust statement.**

> Anthropic: 5 items with Research first and product demoted to a button; Stripe: 5 items with Pricing promoted ('nothing to hide about money'); Linear: 6 items; all three end interior pages with exactly one CTA band, never a CTA zoo.

**16. Two audiences need a structural device, not sequential sprawl or tonal drift. Tokens stay identical across audiences; only surface and voice invert.**

> Stripe serves four audiences in one 31-section scroll and bloats; Notion runs two tones with no device and its /about warmth drifted away from its AI-corporate homepage. AfterDesk's toggle + seam is the device both of them lack — proven necessary by their failures.

## Copy laws (word economy)

**C1. Hero headline ≤8 words; hero subtext ≤14 words and it must contain a mechanism, a time, or a number. Nothing else in the hero.**

> Measured across all five: Ramp 'Time is money. Save both.' (5), Stripe 'Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue.' (6), Notion (6), Linear (8), Anthropic (10, the loosest). Subtexts: Linear 11, Ramp 12, Notion 14, Stripe 18.

**C2. Section headings are 3-7 words, verb-first or two-beat. Body under a heading is one sentence, two absolute max — and that sentence carries a number or a time.**

> Counted: Stripe 'Convert more customers' (3), 'Scale with confidence' (3); Anthropic 'How we hire' (3); Notion 'One search for everything.' (4); Ramp 'Systems that never spoke' (4); Linear 'Define the product direction' (4). Linear and Stripe both cap body at one sentence.

**C3. The two-beat meter is the house sentence: beat one names the pain or the setup, beat two names the win. ≤9 words total. This is the single highest-leverage pattern for impact-per-word.**

> 'Time is money. Save both.' (Ramp), 'Less tracking. More progress.' and 'You assign the tasks. Notion Agent does the work.' (Notion), 'Plan the present. Build the future.' (Linear), 'Scale the team. Shrink the paperwork.' (Ramp). AfterDesk's existing 'America goes to sleep. You wake up to paid work.' already scans in this meter — the rest of the site must match it.

**C4. Every sentence does exactly one of two jobs: name a benefit (≤6 words) or prove it (a number, a name, a timestamp). Any sentence doing neither is deleted.**

> Stripe is the purest case — 'Boost authorisation rates by 2.2% on average'; Ramp's formula is aphorism → mechanism → number; Anthropic's release cards are one sentence each. None of the five sites has a sentence that merely elaborates.

**C5. Zero hype adjectives. 'Powerful', 'seamless', 'effortless' are absent from all five reference sites. Nouns, verbs, and numbers carry all persuasion; one precise word can carry an entire guarantee.**

> Stripe's '2.9% + CA$0.30 per successful transaction' — 'successful' means you only pay when it works, one word doing a paragraph's job. AfterDesk's equivalent word is 'approved'. This law is already in our brand rules; the references prove it is universal at the top tier.

**C6. Homepage total marketing copy budget: ~600 words. The artifacts carry the explanation; copy only names the chapters.**

> Notion's entire homepage runs ~600 words against ~10 screenshots; Anthropic's homepage is 5 sections and a footer; Linear's marketing chrome is a minority of its own page (1 selling section vs 5 showing sections). Stripe's 31 sections are the cautionary counter-example.

**C7. Directly under every price: one reassurance line of ≤8 words, ideally a triple negative. Position matters — it discharges the trust burden at the exact moment of doubt.**

> Stripe: 'No setup fees, monthly fees, or hidden fees.' — 8 words, immediately beneath the number. AfterDesk version: 'No subscriptions, no bidding, no surprises.'

**C8. Caption artifacts like museum exhibits, not explanations. The timestamp or label does the persuading; ≤10 words per caption.**

> Ramp: 'This is what five separate systems to reimburse a flight looks like.' — the problem shown as a labeled specimen. Applied to our CSV centerpiece: before-pane caption 'The file at 11:47 PM.' / after-pane '6:02 AM.' — the two timestamps replace every paragraph currently explaining the transformation.

**C9. Ledger and table rows get ≤8 words per row: date + mono tag + short title + figure. No descriptive sentences inside evidence displays.**

> Anthropic's /news and /research are Date | Tag | Title rows with zero descriptions and they are the site's densest trust engine; Linear's changelog entries are dated one-liners; Ramp's ticker labels are 2-4 uppercase words.

**C10. Bookend: the final section repeats the opening line verbatim plus the one action. No new copy is written for the close.**

> Ramp ends its homepage with the exact hero headline + email field repeated; Stripe closes with a single final CTA. Zero-cost ending, and it enforces that the opening line was strong enough to bear repeating.

## Per-site verdicts

### linear.app

**Steal:** The artifact-as-hero (dense real UI under an 8-word headline), numbered chapters (1.0-5.0) each = one 4-7-word heading + one full-width artifact, the luminance ladder (4 text tints, 4 surface steps, hairlines, no shadows — elevation = lighter surface), weight cap at 600 with size-scaled negative tracking, the changelog-as-trust strip, one entrance then stillness, and the Method-page move: publishing your process doctrine as an authority page.

**Avoid:** The skin. 'Linear design' (dark canvas + glow + glassmorphism) is now the most-cloned look in SaaS — copying the surface is copying a thousand imitators. Also avoid its in-crowd opacity (headline meaningless to newcomers, no explanation path for evaluators) and its near-limit contrast on quaternary gray text.

### ramp.com

**Steal:** The live-ledger identity: odometer counters with tabular-nums as the hero's first pixel, the 1px-gap bento (one rounded container whose grid gap paints hairline seams — literally 'the seam' made structural), the stat-wall grammar (huge number + ≤8-word caption), one accent on exactly 8 elements, problem-as-exhibit captioning, single-typeface-single-weight restraint, compliance fine print worn openly, and the verbatim bookend close.

**Avoid:** Proof overshoot: 9 simultaneous counters blur into noise (use 3-4), 18 testimonials means skimming by quote 12 (use 5-6), the 135-link SEO-calculator footer farm, 10px labels at 60% opacity (aesthetics beating readability), CTA nagging (6+ repeats), and the AI-buzzword pivot ('blink of AI', 'agentic era') that dates an otherwise timeless voice.

### anthropic.com

**Steal:** The tightest copy math on the internet (≤10-word hero, 3-7-word headings, one-sentence cards), proof-by-artifact (dated clickable documents instead of claims), the index-table as trust engine (Date | mono tag | Title), candor as compression (constraints stated plainly read as integrity), the three-voice type system (declare / reflect / annotate-in-mono, ~4 styles total), depth by warm surface tone instead of shadow, tiny nav with one CTA per page, and numbered structure in mono ('01/07').

**Avoid:** Fame-subsidized vagueness — the homepage tells a first-time buyer nothing actionable (no price, no how-it-works); a marketplace without Anthropic's brand equity cannot afford this. Also avoid the 60+-link footer landfill, description-less table rows that force blind clicks, CTAs so quiet the conversion path disappears, and the ivory+serif skin, which Claude's own design tooling has already commoditized.

### notion.com

**Steal:** The two-beat mantra heading ('Less tracking. More progress.' — pain-beat then win-beat, ≤9 words), the ~600-word homepage budget with claim:proof at 1:1, the hyper-specific stat ('98% of the Forbes Cloud 100'), mono uppercase kickers above headings, single-color CTA monopoly, decoration derived from the product's own UI (never invented), elevation-as-meaning (hairline cards; the one deep shadow reserved for the hero artifact), and recommended-state flagged by a 2px border, never by size.

**Avoid:** Identity drift under trend pressure (the 'Think together' capital-T gimmick is their weakest line), enterprise-page bloat where three headings say the same thing, 9 pastel tints (6 too many — tints stop meaning anything), and unattainable-logo proof where every testimonial is Fortune-100 scale and a small buyer finds nobody like themselves. Notion's tonal drift is the proof our seam/toggle device matters.

### stripe.com

**Steal:** The one-line arithmetic price with a guarantee word ('per successful transaction'), the ≤8-word triple-negative under every price, exactly-2-tier pricing with depth pushed into tables below, reliability-as-spec-sheet for money handling, the public 'Prohibited businesses' move (naming limits builds trust), Pricing promoted to top nav as a trust statement, the fixed case-study formula (one sentence + two stats, identical anatomy), docs-as-marketing-surface, and the quarantine principle: one expressive zone, monochrome everywhere else.

**Avoid:** The 31-section mega-scroll serving four audiences sequentially (our toggle is the better answer), category jargon that assumes the reader already speaks the house language ('agentic commerce'), weight-300 body text skirting contrast limits (never on night backgrounds), building a custom WebGL engine for atmosphere (CSS only), and the 80-link footer, which read as fake scale when you don't have 26 products.

## Application map

### P0

Current pages likely run 1,200-1,500 words each — target is a 50-60% cut. Delete every sentence that neither names a benefit (≤6 words) nor proves one (number/name/timestamp). This is the direct answer to the founder's verdict.
  - _Source:_ Notion 600-word budget + Stripe 'name it or prove it' sentence test + Anthropic headline math
- **Client homepage — hero + CSV centerpiece** — Keep 'Describe any task. / Get it back done by morning.' (already two-beat). Cut the sub to ≤14 words. Strip all explanatory prose around the before/after CSV and replace with exhibit captions: before-pane 'The file at 11:47 PM.' (5 words), after-pane '6:02 AM.' (2 words) — the timestamps do the persuading. Any paragraph currently explaining the transformation (likely 40-80 words) goes to zero. The CSV gets the page's only deep shadow so it reads as a physical object.
  - _Source:_ Ramp problem-as-exhibit + Linear artifact-as-hero + Notion mockup-shadow reservation
- **Client homepage — ledger of examples** — Convert from example cards with descriptions to an Anthropic-style index table: Date | Geist Mono uppercase category | ≤6-word task title | fixed price in green tabular numerals. Each row drops from ~20 words to ≤8. Rename the section 'Last night.' (2 words). Rows must be real or clearly-labeled samples — no fabricated entries per honesty rules.
  - _Source:_ Anthropic index-table trust engine + Linear changelog strip
- **Client homepage — quote card** — Render the price as one mono arithmetic expression ('$45 flat. Delete any surrounding pricing explanation (cut to these two lines, ~14 words total).
  - _Source:_ Stripe one-line arithmetic price + triple-negative reassurance placement
- **Both homepages — every claim welded to proof** — Audit every remaining heading: 'done by morning' must sit beside a real turnaround artifact or counter; 'reviewed by the operator' beside the actual QC checklist artifact; any section left without an artifact after the audit is deleted entirely, not rewritten. Add Geist Mono 11px uppercase kickers ('INTAKE', 'REVIEW', 'MORNING') above each section heading — structure without words.
  - _Source:_ Universal claim:proof 1:1 law + Notion product-noun kickers

## Anti-patterns (what the references prove we must NOT do)

- Copying the skin instead of the discipline: the dark-glow 'Linear look' and the ivory-serif 'Anthropic look' are both documented clichés now — AfterDesk's night/paper/seam identity stays, and only the underlying laws transfer.
- Proof overshoot: Ramp's 9 simultaneous counters blur into noise and its 18-quote wall loses the reader by quote 12. AfterDesk: max 3-4 counters, 5-6 proof cards. Past the threshold, more proof subtracts trust.
- The sequential mega-scroll: Stripe's 31 sections serving four audiences in one page is the failure our two-audience toggle exists to prevent. Never let the client page grow worker sections or vice versa.
- Fabricating proof: Notion's Fortune-100-only logos alienate small buyers, and AfterDesk's honesty rules make it worse — no fake logos, no invented testimonials, no counters showing numbers that aren't real. A real $45 task beats an invented enterprise story.
- Readability sacrificed to aesthetics: Ramp's 10px labels at 60% opacity, Stripe's weight-300 body, Linear's near-limit quaternary gray — all documented failures. On the night side keep body ≥400 weight and verify every text tint against WCAG.
- Trend-surfing vocabulary: Ramp's 'blink of AI' and Notion's 'Think together' show how buzzwords date a site and fog a once-crystal value prop. AfterDesk copy uses only timeless words: night, morning, task, price, operator.
- Footer landfill and SEO farms: Ramp's 135 links and calculator farm, Anthropic's 60+ near-duplicate columns, Stripe's 80 links — premature scale reads as fake scale. Cap the footer at ~25 real links.
- Beautiful vagueness: Anthropic's homepage tells a first-time buyer nothing actionable and survives on brand equity AfterDesk does not have. Every page must answer what it costs and how it works within one scroll.
- Decorative sprawl: Notion's 9 pastel tints stopped meaning anything. Any accent must be derived from AfterDesk's own artifacts (status stamps, diff colors) and capped at 3-4 — never an invented decorative palette.
- Heading redundancy under stakeholder pressure: Notion's enterprise page says the same thing three ways in three sections. One idea gets one section; if a new heading restates an existing one, it is a cut, not an addition.
- CTA nagging: Ramp repeats its demo CTA 6+ times per page. One CTA per side, repeated exactly twice (hero and bookend), never more.
- Engineering vanity for atmosphere: Stripe built a custom WebGL engine for its gradient. The seam's hero treatment is CSS only — a one-operator company spends craft on the ledger, not on shader infrastructure.
